Separate fuel savers from fuel myths.

Most real fuel savings come from maintenance and driving habits, not from a bolt-on miracle. A few inexpensive tools do help because they let you measure and correct the basics.

A tyre gauge is the best-value fuel tool

Underinflated tyres increase rolling resistance, wear out early and change handling. Checking pressures when the tyres are cold is one of the few 'fuel gadgets' with an immediate, measurable payoff.

Use the vehicle's own specification, not the number on the tyre sidewall, and check monthly rather than after a long drive.

Fix a fault before buying an additive

A check engine light, rough idle or rich running can waste far more fuel than any additive can save. A basic code reader identifies the fault so you can repair the cause instead of masking a symptom.

Once the engine is healthy, clean air filters, correct oil grade and proper tyre pressures do more for economy than most bottles and magnets.

Measure before and after

Track fuel used over several full tanks, not one doubtful fill. Use the same route, similar speeds and the same pump where possible. Our fuel-saving page includes a calculator and a printable tracker.

If a product cannot show a repeatable change in your own records, the claim is not worth paying for.

See the fuel-saving card guide →

Claims to treat with scepticism

Magnetic fuel conditioners, single-wire 'performance chips', intake vortices and unsupported additive promises rarely survive a controlled before-and-after test. Be especially wary of anything that asks you to ignore the owner's manual.

Spend first on maintenance, correct driving habits and diagnosis. That approach saves fuel without risking the engine, warranty or emissions system.
